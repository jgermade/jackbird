A home server, a NAS, a Raspberry Pi

Docker Compose

mkdir jackbird && cd jackbird
curl -fsSLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jgermade/jackbird/main/deploy/compose.yaml
curl -fsSL -o .env https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jgermade/jackbird/main/deploy/.env.example
mkdir -p config library
$EDITOR .env
docker compose up -d

Then docker compose exec jackbird jackbird-service setup for sign-in and the first owner. The image is ghcr.io/jgermade/jackbird (x64 and arm64).

What it does

Play

Your folder, laid out as albums

Open a folder of MP3 and FLAC files and it shows your albums with their covers, discs and tracks, and plays them gaplessly from where they lie.

Edit

Tags, one track or many

Title, artist, album, year, track numbers. Select three tracks or thirty and fix what they share in one go. It writes ID3 and Vorbis comments itself.

Covers

Artwork that sticks

Drop an image on an album and it goes into every file of it, so the cover follows the music to any player.

Private

Nothing is uploaded

No account, no server, no sync. The browser asks once for permission to edit the folder; your files never leave it.

Deck

Built like the gear you miss

Keys that travel, a dot-matrix display, labels printed on the faceplate, lettering in chrome. Two tones: Silver and Graphite.

Family

One library for the house

Run it as a service on a NAS or a Raspberry Pi: people, roles, sign-in by email and passkeys, and every browser at home as its front panel.

In your pocket

The same deck on a phone, with the queue playing in the background, screen off.

On Android it is an app that opens a folder on the phone and plays it natively. Everywhere else, the web app scales to the width of your hand.
