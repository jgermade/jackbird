# AAC and Apple Lossless (`.m4a`): read, write, copy, play

## 2026-11-28 — Why, and the plan (proposal; awaiting the owner's approval)

**Why.** The owner's nano 7G holds 1759 songs: 1468 AAC (`.m4a`, about 256 kbps, iTunes
purchases by the look of them; `afinfo`) and 291 MP3. jackbird lists and plays an iPod's AAC songs
(Apple's library, from its database), but everything else is MP3 and FLAC only. The tag engine
reads and writes those two (`crates/tags`); the family library imports only those two
(`crates/store` `import_file`); a folder's scan, the drop and "Copy to…" take only those two
(`helpers/formats.ts` `AUDIO_FORMATS`). So five of six songs on the nano cannot be copied to My
music. Since 2026-11-28 "Copy to…" leaves them out and says how many
(RECORD/2026-11-03.deck-design-system.WIP.md, same date).

**The goal.** An `.m4a` (AAC or Apple Lossless) is a track like an MP3 or FLAC: its tags read and
written by jackbird's own engine, its audio never touched, in a folder, in the library, dropped,
copied, played by the desktop app's native output and listed in Rockbox's library.

**Out of it.** Protected files (`.m4p`); video (`.m4v`, `.mp4`); converting between formats;
sending AAC to an iPod (its own step later, see the end); audiobooks' chapters.

### The format, and what makes it harder than ID3 or FLAC

- An MPEG-4 file is a tree of boxes (atoms): `ftyp`, `moov` (the description: tracks, sample
  tables, and the tags under `moov/udta/meta/ilst`), `mdat` (the audio), `free` (padding).
- **The tags are not at the head.** `moov` may come before `mdat` ("fast start", what iTunes
  writes) or after it. The engine's model today is "a head that grows, and the last bytes"
  (`crates/tags` `layout`); for MP4 the layout walks the top-level boxes by their headers, which
  takes reads at chosen offsets, not only a head.
- **Writing moves the audio's offsets.** The sample tables (`stco`, 32-bit, or `co64`) hold
  absolute file offsets into `mdat`. If `moov` is before `mdat` and grows or shrinks, every offset
  shifts by the difference. The plan, in order of preference:
  1. the new `ilst` fits by taking from or giving to a `free` box right after it (or inside
     `meta`/`udta`): no byte of the audio and no offset moves (iTunes leaves such padding);
  2. else `moov` is rewritten whole with every `stco`/`co64` entry shifted (a `stco` entry that
     would overflow 32 bits becomes a `co64` box), and 2 KiB of `free` padding is left for the
     next save;
  3. with `moov` after `mdat`, it is rewritten in place at the end and nothing shifts.
- **The audio check stays.** As for MP3 and FLAC, a save is refused unless the audio is the same
  bytes: here `mdat`'s content (SHA-1), and every sample offset pointing at the same bytes of it.
  That is also the audio checksum `.meta/` keeps (E-D5's definitions, extended).

### The tags (`ilst`) and how they map to jackbird's fields

`©nam` title · `©ART` artist · `aART` album artist · `©alb` album · `©day` year · `trkn` track
(and total) · `disk` disc (and total) · `©gen`/`gnre` genre · `cpil` compilation · `covr` cover
(JPEG or PNG) · `©cmt` comment · `©wrt` composer · `tmpo` BPM · `----:com.apple.iTunes:<name>`
freeform (the user tags, as ID3's `TXXX` and FLAC's comments are). Atoms jackbird does not know are
kept byte for byte. Duration and sample rate from `mdhd`; codec (AAC `mp4a`, Apple Lossless
`alac`) and bitrate from `stsd`/`esds`.

## 2026-11-28 — A1: how reading goes in (the owner: "adelante con A1")

- **`crates/mp4`**, a tag crate like `id3` and `flac` (no I/O, no dependencies): box headers (32-
  and 64-bit sizes, a size of 0 to the end), the top-level walk, `moov`'s children, `udta/meta/ilst`
  (`meta` as a full box, or QuickTime's plain one, told by whether `hdlr` follows at once), each
  item with its `data` atoms (type, locale, value) and, for `----`, its `mean` and `name`, and where
  each item lies, which A2 keeps byte for byte. The audio track: its sample entry (`mp4a` with
  `esds`: AAC's object type and bit rates; `alac` with its magic cookie: bit depth), sample rate,
  channels, `mdhd`'s duration and time scale.
- **`crates/tags`**: `Format::Mp4`; `format_of` by `ftyp` at offset 4; `src/mp4.rs` maps the items
  to the fields:
  - `©nam` title, `©ART` artist, `©alb` album, `aART` album artist, `©wrt` composer, `©cmt` comment,
    `©lyr` lyrics; several `data` atoms in one item joined by `/`, as ID3's several values are.
  - `©day` the year **as the file holds it** (iTunes' `2001-05-04T07:00:00Z` included), as ID3's
    `TDRC` is: the interface already shows the first four digits of a date (`yearOfTracks`,
    `yearOrder`), and a save of an unchanged year keeps the whole date. This is the owner's "show
    the year, keep the date" with no conversion.
  - `©gen` the genre, else `gnre` (ID3v1's number plus one) by its name. ID3v1's table moves from
    `crates/ipod-sync/src/genres.rs` to `crates/id3` (`genre_name`), which both use.
  - `trkn`, `disk`: `n/total`, or `n` when the total is 0; empty when the number is 0.
  - `cpil` 1 is `1`; 0 or none is empty. `tmpo` its number; 0 is empty.
  - Freeform `----:com.apple.iTunes:<name>` (names as Picard writes them, compared without case):
    `MusicBrainz Album Id`, `MusicBrainz Release Group Id`, `MusicBrainz Album Artist Id`,
    `MusicBrainz Release Track Id`, `MusicBrainz Track Id` (the recording), and, as ID3's `TXXX`
    names them, `DISCOGS_RELEASE_ID` and `jackbird track_id`.
  - The cover: `covr`'s first `data` (13 JPEG, 14 PNG, 27 BMP; 0 by its bytes).
- **Where the tags are (`layout`).** iTunes' layout (`moov` before `mdat`) fits the engine as it is:
  `tags_end` is where `mdat`'s content starts (`ftyp`, `moov`, any `free`, `mdat`'s header), so a
  save replaces that head and the audio follows it untouched. A file whose `mdat` comes first asks
  for the whole file and has `tags_end` at its end, a whole-file save; asking for a range at the end
  instead is A3's. The audio region is `mdat`'s content (the first `mdat`).
- **`audio`**: the duration from `mdhd`, the rates and channels from the sample entry, ALAC's bits;
  the bit rate is the audio's bytes over the duration.
- **Writing is A2**: until then a save of an `.m4a` is refused (`not-supported`).
- **What must change with the new format**: `crates/tags-wasm`'s wire (MP4 is format 2),
  `crates/store` (the extension it gives an imported file: `m4a`).
- **Tests**: `crates/tags/tests/fixtures_m4a.rs` over `cases-m4a.json` (every case reads as said,
  its region, the layout from any head, the audio), unit tests in `crates/mp4`, and an ignored test
  that reads every `.m4a` of a folder given by `JACKBIRD_M4A_CORPUS` (the nano, on the owner's
  Mac).

## 2026-11-28 — A2: how saving goes in (the owner: "go ahead")

**What a save rewrites.** Only `moov`: its `udta/meta/ilst` rebuilt, its chunk offsets shifted when
the audio moves. With `moov` first, the splice is the head (`ftyp`, `moov`, any `free`, `mdat`'s
header), as for MP3 and FLAC; with `moov` last, the whole file (A1's layout).

**The items.** Each item a change does not touch stays byte for byte, in its place. A changed field's
items (all its places: `©gen` and `gnre` for the genre) are taken out and its new item goes where
the first of them was, or at the end; an empty value only takes them out. The new items: text as one
UTF-8 `data`; `trkn` and `disk` as numbers (a value that is not `n` or `n/total` is refused);
`cpil` as the integer 1; `tmpo` as a 16-bit integer (not a number: refused); the ids as freeform
`----:com.apple.iTunes:<name>`. The cover: `covr`'s first picture replaced (JPEG 13, PNG 14, BMP 27),
its other pictures kept; or a new `covr`; removing takes out that picture, and `covr` when none is
left. No `udta`, `meta` or `ilst`: they are made, `meta` as iTunes' full box with its `mdir`
handler.

**Where the room comes from, in order:**

1. the `free` right after `ilst` in `meta` takes the difference (iTunes' 512 KiB on the nano's
   files): `moov` keeps its size and nothing moves;
2. else, with `moov` first, a `free` right after `moov` (as `afconvert` leaves 3 KiB) takes it:
   `moov` changes size, the audio does not move;
3. else `moov` changes size with 2 KiB of `free` after `ilst` for the next save, and, with `moov`
   first, every chunk offset (`stco`, `co64`, in every track) past `moov` shifts by the difference.
   A 32-bit offset that would overflow is refused (`too-large`; not met in songs); a fragmented
   file (`mvex`) or one with `saio` offsets is refused when its audio would move (`unreadable`).
   A shrinking `moov` is the same rule: the room left over becomes `free` when it is 8 bytes or
   more.

**The safeguards, for MP4:**

1. the result reads back as asked (as for every format);
2. the new head ends exactly where its own boxes say the audio starts (`mdat`'s content, or the
   file's end with `moov` last), and on a whole file the audio (`mdat`'s content) is the same
   bytes, and with `moov` first so is all that follows it;
3. kept: every top-level box but `moov` and the `free` after it, byte for byte; every child of
   `moov` but `udta`, byte for byte except the chunk offsets, which must point at the same bytes of
   the audio as before (each offset less the audio's start, the same); `udta`'s other children,
   `meta`'s version and handler; every item a change does not touch, in order; and `covr`'s other
   pictures.

**Tests.** The fixtures: a new title on every case; every field at once; emptied fields; a new
cover and a removed one; no change giving the same bytes; the head-and-splice path giving the whole
file's bytes; `m4a-faststart-padded` keeping its size, `m4a-faststart-tight` and `m4a-co64`
shifting their offsets. Each save's samples, taken out by the test's own walk of the sample tables,
are the source's. lofty (already a dev-dependency) reads what jackbird writes. On the nano, an
ignored test saves each file's head with new tags in memory, and writes a few whole copies to a
temporary folder for `afinfo` and `ffmpeg` to play.
