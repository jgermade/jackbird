# AAC and Apple Lossless (`.m4a`): read, write, copy, play

## 2026-11-28 — Why, and the plan (proposal; awaiting the owner's approval)

**Why.** The owner's nano 7G holds 1759 songs: 1468 AAC (`.m4a`, about 256 kbps, iTunes
purchases by the look of them; `afinfo`) and 291 MP3. jackbird lists and plays an iPod's AAC songs
(Apple's library, from its database), but everything else is MP3 and FLAC only. The tag engine
reads and writes those two (`crates/tags`); the family library imports only those two
(`crates/store` `import_file`); a folder's scan, the drop and "Copy to…" take only those two
(`helpers/formats.ts` `AUDIO_FORMATS`). So five of six songs on the nano cannot be copied to My
music. Since 2026-11-28 "Copy to…" leaves them out and says how many
(RECORD/2026-11-03.deck-design-system.WIP.md, same date).

**The goal.** An `.m4a` (AAC or Apple Lossless) is a track like an MP3 or FLAC: its tags read and
written by jackbird's own engine, its audio never touched, in a folder, in the library, dropped,
copied, played by the desktop app's native output and listed in Rockbox's library.

**Out of it.** Protected files (`.m4p`); video (`.m4v`, `.mp4`); converting between formats;
sending AAC to an iPod (its own step later, see the end); audiobooks' chapters.

### The format, and what makes it harder than ID3 or FLAC

- An MPEG-4 file is a tree of boxes (atoms): `ftyp`, `moov` (the description: tracks, sample
  tables, and the tags under `moov/udta/meta/ilst`), `mdat` (the audio), `free` (padding).
- **The tags are not at the head.** `moov` may come before `mdat` ("fast start", what iTunes
  writes) or after it. The engine's model today is "a head that grows, and the last bytes"
  (`crates/tags` `layout`); for MP4 the layout walks the top-level boxes by their headers, which
  takes reads at chosen offsets, not only a head.
- **Writing moves the audio's offsets.** The sample tables (`stco`, 32-bit, or `co64`) hold
  absolute file offsets into `mdat`. If `moov` is before `mdat` and grows or shrinks, every offset
  shifts by the difference. The plan, in order of preference:
  1. the new `ilst` fits by taking from or giving to a `free` box right after it (or inside
     `meta`/`udta`): no byte of the audio and no offset moves (iTunes leaves such padding);
  2. else `moov` is rewritten whole with every `stco`/`co64` entry shifted (a `stco` entry that
     would overflow 32 bits becomes a `co64` box), and 2 KiB of `free` padding is left for the
     next save;
  3. with `moov` after `mdat`, it is rewritten in place at the end and nothing shifts.
- **The audio check stays.** As for MP3 and FLAC, a save is refused unless the audio is the same
  bytes: here `mdat`'s content (SHA-1), and every sample offset pointing at the same bytes of it.
  That is also the audio checksum `.meta/` keeps (E-D5's definitions, extended).

### The tags (`ilst`) and how they map to jackbird's fields

`©nam` title · `©ART` artist · `aART` album artist · `©alb` album · `©day` year · `trkn` track
(and total) · `disk` disc (and total) · `©gen`/`gnre` genre · `cpil` compilation · `covr` cover
(JPEG or PNG) · `©cmt` comment · `©wrt` composer · `tmpo` BPM · `----:com.apple.iTunes:<name>`
freeform (the user tags, as ID3's `TXXX` and FLAC's comments are). Atoms jackbird does not know are
kept byte for byte. Duration and sample rate from `mdhd`; codec (AAC `mp4a`, Apple Lossless
`alac`) and bitrate from `stsd`/`esds`.

## 2026-11-28 — A6 revised: AAC already plays in the desktop app (the owner)

**The owner.** The `.m4a` files sound in the desktop app on macOS.

**Why.** The native player (`crates/audio`, Symphonia built with `mp3` and `flac` only) cannot open
them, so it sends `fallback` (`lib/player.ts`), and the embedded output (the webview's `<audio>`;
WebKit decodes AAC and ALAC through the system) takes over. So playing them needs nothing.

**What A6 still brings,** lower in the order: the native output's own ways for these songs, that
is, exclusive (bit-perfect) output, the same device as the other songs, gapless with the songs
around them, and no switch of output at each AAC song. A6 moves to the end, optional.

## 2026-11-28 — The test audio: generated here, and the nano as a local corpus (the owner: "whatever is most reliable")

Three layers, each catching what the others cannot:

1. **Committed fixtures built byte by byte** (`fixtures/tags/make.mjs`, as for MP3 and FLAC): a
   generated tone encoded by `ffmpeg` (its `aac` and `alac` encoders), and every box around it
   (`ftyp`, `moov` before and after `mdat`, `free` or none, `stco` and `co64`, `ilst` with each
   field, a cover, freeform and unknown atoms) written by the script. The expectations come from
   how each file is built, never from the code under test. Small, reproducible, no one's
   recording, and CI (Linux) needs no Apple tool.
2. **One committed file as Apple's tools make it**: the same tone through macOS's `afconvert`
   (`m4af`), kept as made, so a layout no hand-built fixture thought of is still covered (as
   `make.mjs` keeps one MP3 "as the app saved before E2").
3. **The nano's 1468 purchases, never committed**: a test run by hand on the owner's Mac
   (ignored in CI) reads every `.m4a` of a mounted iPod, and, on copies in a temporary folder,
   saves new tags and checks that the audio is the same bytes and that `afinfo` still opens each.
   This is what iTunes' own files (`iTunSMPB`, sort atoms, purchase atoms, their padding) are
   checked against.

The order stays A0–A5, then A7; A6 last and optional.

## 2026-11-28 — A0 done: the `.m4a` fixtures (the owner's go-ahead: "dale")

- **Branch.** `feat/aac-m4a`, from `fix/copy-to-library-server` (which holds this record).
- **A script of its own.** `fixtures/tags/make-m4a.mjs`, not `make.mjs`: run with the `ffmpeg` here
  (9.1, from Homebrew), `make.mjs` rewrote all 26 MP3 and FLAC fixtures and their covers with other
  bytes (another encoder version). So the `.m4a` fixtures are made apart, reuse the committed covers,
  and are listed in their own `cases-m4a.json`, which none of today's readers of `cases.json` (the
  tag crates' and core's tests, the wasm engine's, the audio crate's, `fixtures/audio/make.mjs`)
  sees until A1 reads it.
- **How each is made.** `ffmpeg` encodes the tone (AAC 32 kbps, and ALAC; 22050 Hz mono); the
  script takes its boxes that describe the audio as they are (`mvhd`, `tkhd`, `edts`, `mdhd`, the
  handler, `stsd`, `stts`, `stsz`, the sample groups) and its samples, found through the sample
  tables, and writes the rest: `ftyp`; the chunks (`stsc`, then `stco` or `co64`, worked out once
  `moov`'s size is known); `udta`/`meta`/`ilst` atom by atom; `free`; `mdat`.
- **The thirteen cases.** iTunes' layout with every field and 1 KiB of `free` in `meta`; the same
  with no `free`; with 2 KiB of `free` between `moov` and `mdat`; `moov` last; `co64` with chunks of
  three; a 64-bit `mdat` size; no `udta`; an empty `ilst`; QuickTime's `meta`; ALAC with a PNG and
  a track and disc without totals; `gnre` 18 (Rock) and `cpil` 0; UTF-8 beyond Latin-1; and the
  file `afconvert` made, kept as made. The full list also holds two covers, freeform atoms
  (MusicBrainz' track id, `iTunSMPB`, a ReplayGain value), and atoms jackbird has no field for
  (`©too`, `soar`, `apID`, an unknown `xqzw`), which a save must keep.
- **`afconvert` writes one atom.** `----:com.apple.iTunes:iTunSMPB` (the gapless information), and
  3 KiB of `free` before `mdat`. The script fails if it ever finds any other atom there, since the
  case expects no field.
- **Checked against readers that are not jackbird's**:
  - `afinfo` (Apple's) opens all thirteen: AAC or ALAC, 22050 Hz, 0.5 s.
  - Decoded by `ffmpeg`, every built file gives the same PCM as the reference encode (MD5), so
    every chunk offset points at the right bytes in each layout.
  - `ffprobe` reads every expected field in every case, except the BPM, which its reader does not
    show; the `tmpo` atom is byte for byte what `ffmpeg` itself writes for 120.
  - The covers `ffmpeg` takes out are `cover1.jpg` and `cover3.png` (SHA-1).
- **Not in A0, for later cases.** iTunes' dates (`©day` as `2001-05-04T07:00:00Z`): what jackbird's
  year shows for them is decided in A1, against the nano's files. Fragmented MP4 (`moof`), which a
  save should refuse: its error code is A2's.
